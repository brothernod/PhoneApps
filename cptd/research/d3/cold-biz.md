### Case case-biz-onboard: Making the case for faster onboarding

You are the TD director at a regional bank. Branch managers say new tellers take too long to become productive, and the COO asks whether TD can help. Funding decisions for next year are due in two months.

#### case-biz-onboard.step-1

Before proposing any program, which actions fit Newbauer’s framework and ATD’s advice on barriers?

1. Start designing learning activities so you have something to show the COO
2. Assume a course is the answer, since the COO came to TD
3. Identify the business challenge and translate it into a business goal
4. Look for root causes, such as unclear expectations or work systems, before choosing training
5. Check whether learning is an appropriate strategy for reaching that goal

#### case-biz-onboard.step-2

You map the stakeholders for your proposal on a power/interest grid. Which treatments fit?

1. Branch trainers: ignore them, since they have no power over the decision
2. The CFO: send her every weekly status update in full detail
3. The COO: a monthly newsletter is enough
4. The CFO, who can block the funding but has little interest in details: keep satisfied
5. Branch trainers, who care a lot but have little say: keep informed
6. The COO, who sponsors the effort and is closely involved: manage closely

#### case-biz-onboard.step-3

You write the business case. Which choices are sound?

1. Make NPV and IRR the headline measures, since Phillips says they suit TD best
2. Use economic, financial, and organizational data, such as the local labor market, costs and projected benefits, and teller turnover
3. Open with an executive summary
4. Emphasize business and operational impact, not just the rollout timeline
5. Report a payback period, one of the three measures Phillips says work for most investments
6. Leave costs out until after the program is approved

#### case-biz-onboard.step-4

When you present your findings, a regional VP keeps asking for more data before agreeing to anything, and the CFO says there’s no time for this right now. Your analysis also shows that the bank’s incentive plan rewards speed over accuracy. What should you do?

1. Partner with HR and branch leadership on the incentive plan, since training alone won’t fix it
2. Skip agreement and start implementing to show momentum
3. Agree on goals and action steps with the COO before implementing anything
4. Promise that the training will fix the incentive problem too
5. Recognize the VP’s and CFO’s reactions as faces of resistance, which Block expects when data is fed back
6. Drop the proposal, since resistance means the case has failed
