### Case case-design-safety: The incident-reporting program

You are an instructional designer. A new incident-reporting procedure requires frontline supervisors to file a detailed, multi-step report after any safety incident and to talk with employees who are reluctant to report. Incidents are rare, and errors in a report can lead to regulatory fines.

#### case-design-safety.step-1

You draft outcome statements. Which are well-formed learning or behavioral outcome statements?

1. The facilitator will present the new procedure in a 45-minute lecture
2. Supervisors will understand the importance of incident reporting
3. Given the incident form and a sample scenario, supervisors will complete every required field with no errors
4. Supervisors will appreciate the value of the new form
5. Within 30 days of training, supervisors will file a complete report for every incident on their shift

#### case-design-safety.step-2

The program covers the policy facts and form fields, plus practice with tense conversations with reluctant employees. Which delivery choices fit?

1. Deliver the whole program as recorded video, since it is cheapest
2. Assign a short asynchronous module on the policy and form as prep before a live session
3. Spend the live session reading the policy aloud and skip the practice
4. Treat the virtual ILT as asynchronous so supervisors can attend whenever they like
5. Hold the conversation practice synchronously, live or as virtual ILT

#### case-design-safety.step-3

You plan the asynchronous module. The stakeholders are new to e-learning, and the legal team expects to change much of the wording. How should you plan it?

1. Make the storyboard blend content, assets, instruction, and navigation
2. Write the storyboard after development, as a record of what was built
3. Skip planning documents and build the refined digital version right away
4. Keep separate documents for the instructional design and the visual design
5. Write a storyboard first, since written content is easier to revise
6. Show the stakeholders a rapid prototype full of placeholder images and ask them to approve the content

#### case-design-safety.step-4

Now you design the learning assets. Which choices fit the sources?

1. Mark required fields on the job aid in red only
2. Give non-players observer sheets listing behaviors to record during the role plays
3. Give supervisors the job aid instead of any training, since they have never filed this report
4. Create a job aid, such as a checklist, for filing the report, since the task is rare, complex, and high-consequence
5. Add the history of the reporting policy to the job aid for context
6. Use structured role playing with a written case and roles to practice the reluctant-employee conversation
