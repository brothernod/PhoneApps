752. [case-design-safety.step-1.opt-4] In step 1 of case-design-safety, “The facilitator will present the new procedure in a 45-minute lecture” is a WRONG pick.

754. [case-design-safety.step-1.why] Explanation for step 1 of case-design-safety: The first has a condition (“Given the incident form…”), an observable performance, and a criterion (“no errors”); the second is an application objective about on-the-job behavior. “Understand” and “appreciate” aren’t observable, and the lecture describes a process, not an intended outcome.

757. [case-design-safety.step-2.opt-2] In step 2 of case-design-safety, “Hold the conversation practice synchronously, live or as virtual ILT” is a RIGHT pick.
758. [case-design-safety.step-2.opt-3] In step 2 of case-design-safety, “Deliver the whole program as recorded video, since it is cheapest” is a WRONG pick.
759. [case-design-safety.step-2.opt-4] In step 2 of case-design-safety, “Treat the virtual ILT as asynchronous so supervisors can attend whenever they like” is a WRONG pick.
760. [case-design-safety.step-2.opt-5] In step 2 of case-design-safety, “Spend the live session reading the policy aloud and skip the practice” is a WRONG pick.
761. [case-design-safety.step-2.why] Explanation for step 2 of case-design-safety: Lower-level facts suit asynchronous delivery, which also makes good prep for a live session; interaction-heavy practice suits synchronous. Virtual ILT is synchronous by definition, and recordings alone give up the interaction the conversations need.

767. [case-design-safety.step-3.opt-5] In step 3 of case-design-safety, “Write the storyboard after development, as a record of what was built” is a WRONG pick.
768. [case-design-safety.step-3.why] Explanation for step 3 of case-design-safety: The ATD Blog suggests a storyboard when stakeholders are new to e-learning (they comment on placeholder images) or many content edits are expected, and advises keeping at least two documents, one for instructional design and one for visual design. The storyboard is the blueprint, so it comes before building, not after.

773. [case-design-safety.step-4.opt-4] In step 4 of case-design-safety, “Give supervisors the job aid instead of any training, since they have never filed this report” is a WRONG pick.
774. [case-design-safety.step-4.opt-5] In step 4 of case-design-safety, “Add the history of the reporting policy to the job aid for context” is a WRONG pick.
775. [case-design-safety.step-4.opt-6] In step 4 of case-design-safety, “Mark required fields on the job aid in red only” is a WRONG pick.
776. [case-design-safety.step-4.why] Explanation for step 4 of case-design-safety: Low frequency, high complexity, and high consequence of error make filing the report a strong job-aid candidate, and structured role play with observer sheets suits practicing a job skill. But job aids remind people of tasks already learned, so they don’t replace training; the policy history breaks “eliminate irrelevancies”; and color alone breaks “make it accessible.”
