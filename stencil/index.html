Layered stencil maker

Turn a photo into a stack of spray-paint stencils, one per color. Paint them in order from lightest to darkest, and download each layer as an SVG for Cricut Design Space.

The sample image is shown. PNGs with a transparent background work best, since the background won't get painted.

Stencil style

Each stencil also opens up every darker area. Later coats cover small gaps, so alignment is forgiving.

The zip includes every layer, the combined file and a paint guide.

Layers, in painting order

Tap a swatch to set it to the paint you actually own. Tap a stencil to preview the board at that step. In the stencils, the colored areas get cut out and the gray is stencil material.

How to paint it

  1. Lightest first. Spray layer 1, let it dry, then lay down layer 2, and so on. In stacked style, layer 1 covers the whole image, so you can spray it freehand as a base coat.
  2. Islands. Islands are pieces that sit loose inside a cut (the middle of an O, the white of an eye). Cut on adhesive stencil vinyl such as Oramask 813 and apply it with transfer tape, so the islands stay where they belong. If you use mylar or cardstock, add thin bridges by hand in Design Space. Each card shows how many islands that layer has.
  3. Registration. Every layer has the same four corner squares. After laying down layer 1, pencil through the squares onto the board, then line each later sheet up to those marks.
  4. In Design Space, upload the SVG and check that the size matches the size shown here. The combined SVG brings in every layer as its own color, so Cricut puts each layer on its own mat. A 12×24 in mat cuts about 11.5×23.5 in. For anything bigger, use smaller art or split it into sections.
  5. Mist coats. Several light passes bleed under the edges less than one heavy coat. Fewer layers and more speck removal make stencils that are faster to weed.
